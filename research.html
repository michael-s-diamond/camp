Research

Clouds confound our understanding of climate change.

We use aircraft observations, satellite geostatistics, and modeling that spans from the cloud scale to the global scale to understand how clouds and aerosols interact and what that means for Earth's climate.

Marine Cloud Brightening

Marine cloud brightening (MCB) involves seeding marine clouds with sea-salt particles to increase their reflectivity and produce a cooling effect. We treat MCB as a possible temporary supplement to decarbonization — not a substitute for it — and study the physical science checkpoints and exit ramps that should guide MCB research.

Diamond, M., A. Gettelman, M. Lebsock, A. McComiskey, L. Russell, R. Wood, & G. Feingold (2022). "Physical science checkpoints and exit ramps for marine cloud brightening research." PNAS, 119(4), e2118379119. doi:10.1073/pnas.2118379119

Transdisciplinary research

Assessing MCB responsibly requires more than atmospheric science — it needs technology, ecology, social science, and ethics working together. The same 2022 PNAS paper above lays out a Sankey-diagram framework for how these disciplines connect.

Natural Experiments in Aerosol-Cloud Interactions

We use shipping and the COVID-19 lockdowns as natural experiments to separate the effect of aerosols on clouds from the effect of meteorological variability.

Shipping effects on marine clouds

We made the first regional-scale detection of ship-pollution cloud brightening, estimating that pollution-driven cloud changes have offset roughly a third of greenhouse-gas warming since pre-industrial times.

Diamond, M., H. Director, R. Eastman, A. Possner, & R. Wood (2020). "Substantial Cloud Brightening from Shipping in Subtropical Low Clouds." AGU Advances, 1(1), e2019AV000111. doi:10.1029/2019AV000111

Pollution changes due to COVID-19

During the February 2020 China shutdown, NO₂ dropped by roughly 50%, but we found no detectable effect on aerosol or cloud microphysics — transport emissions fell by about 90% while industry and power emissions fell by only about 20%.

Diamond, M., & R. Wood (2020). "Limited Regional Aerosol and Cloud Microphysical Changes Despite Unprecedented Decline in Nitrogen Oxide Pollution During the February 2020 COVID-19 Shutdown in China." GRL, 47, e2020GL088913. doi:10.1029/2020GL088913

Smoke-Cloud Interactions

Our focus region is the southeast Atlantic, where biomass-burning smoke from southern Africa interacts with the marine stratocumulus deck. We combine field-campaign observations with modeling to understand these interactions.

Role of drizzle in cloud transitions

The stratocumulus-to-cumulus transition (SCT) can proceed through the classic "deepening-warming" pathway, or through a "drizzle-depletion" pathway we identified — a rain/aerosol-depletion feedback. We're coordinating an international model intercomparison project on this topic.

Smoke-circulation interactions and cloud transitions

Smoke's absorption of sunlight alters large-scale circulation and reduces subsidence, which turns out to have a bigger effect on clouds than smoke's direct cloud-seeding effect.

Diamond, M., P. Saide, P. Zuidema, et al. (2022). "Cloud adjustments from large-scale smoke-circulation interactions strongly modulate the southeast Atlantic stratocumulus-to-cumulus transition." ACP, 22, 12113–12151. doi:10.5194/acp-22-12113-2022

Smoke-cloud interactions in the ORACLES campaign

CCN–Nd correlations are strong below cloud but weak above cloud, because smoke takes days to mix downward. This argues for Lagrangian tracking of air masses over snapshot-style satellite methods.

Pennypacker, S., M. Diamond, & R. Wood (2020). ACP, 20, 2341–2351. · Diamond, M., et al. (2018). ACP, 18(19), 14623–14636.

Cloud Droplet Remote Sensing

Cloud droplet number concentration (CDNC) can't be retrieved directly by satellite — it has to be estimated from other cloud properties. We work on validating and improving these estimates.

Grosvenor, D., et al., including M. Diamond (2018). "Remote sensing of droplet number concentration in warm clouds: A review of the current state of knowledge and perspectives." Reviews of Geophysics, 56, 409–453. doi:10.1029/2017RG000593

Previous Work

Earlier in my career I worked on glacial lake outburst floods and ENSO. These aren't active research areas for me anymore, but I'm always happy to discuss them.

El Niño diversity

We showed that EP/CP El Niño classification is highly dataset-dependent, which undermines prior findings linking El Niño "type" to extratropical weather.

Diamond, M., & R. Bennartz (2015). "Occurrence and trends of eastern and central Pacific El Niño in different reconstructed SST data sets." GRL, 42(23), 10375–10381. doi:10.1002/2015GL066469

Tsangpo megafloods

Glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs) on the Yarlung-Tsangpo may have shaped the Brahmaputra-Jamuna paleovalley — a story that also connects to local legend of Guru Rinpoche.

Pickering, J., M. Diamond, S. Goodbred, et al. (2018). GSA Bulletin, 131(1–2), 58–70. doi:10.1130/B31941.1
